since-clock

An instrument you can take apart.

One .js file, one .css file, no dependencies, no build step. Every frame is Date.now() - start — nothing is ever incremented, so no frame it paints is ever wrong. Click the clock below and rebuild it while it runs.

since 27 May 2026

Change the shell, the units, the colour, the epoch — mid-second, mid-drag, as often as you like. It will not blink, restart, or show you a zero, because there is no counter in here to reset. That is the entire argument of this component, and the console exists mostly to let you try to break it.

The idea

const elapsed = Date.now() - start;

A tally that animates is briefly wrong. Counting up from zero means every frame before the last shows a figure that was never true, and a screenshot caught mid-flight is a lie about a real number. This is the opposite case, and only because of how it is written: nothing is ever incremented and nothing animates toward a value. There is no accumulator, so there is no first frame showing zero, no drift, and no catch-up after the tab was hidden. Miss a thousand frames and the next one is still exactly right.

Hide this tab for a while and come back. The reading is correct, and it never had to work out what it missed.

Motion may dress a clock. It may never falsify one.

The nine effects in the console are adapted from motion-kit, and the adaptation had a rule to obey. shimmer-text is allowed on the numerals: it changes their colour and not their content. text-scramble-hover is not, because a clock cycling random glyphs is a clock displaying readings that were never true — the one thing this component exists to never do. It dresses the console's own headings instead, which are words rather than measurements. Hover one.

The same test decided the rest. Effects that touch the reading have to leave it true; effects that dress the plate, the ring or the panel are unrestricted.

      data-since-shell="rail" data-since-hover="scrub">
  <span data-since-value>since 27 May 2026</span>
</time>

Scrub is the engine's own idea, one level up. Flow mode reads a fractional second nobody asked for; scrub reads a fractional day you pick. Both exist because the engine was never "the time since start, ticking" — it was always "the time between start and any instant," and the instant only had to stop being hardcoded to now. A setInterval clock can do neither.

Left completely alone, fallback intact, no underline. A clock that cannot tell the time should say nothing, not NaN.

What the console is not

It is not the component. since-clock.js and since-clock.css are two files with no dependencies and no build step, and nothing on this page is required to use them — studio.js and studio.css are an optional layer that one element opts into with data-since-studio. Copy the markup the console gives you and you get exactly what you configured, with none of this.
